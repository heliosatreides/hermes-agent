[kent walters's macbook filing post](https://kentwalters.com/posts/corners/) is funny because it starts as a joke and ends as a design review.

the easy take is that people will customize anything. the better take is that a sharp edge only gets filed off when a product has already crossed the line from object to infrastructure. once a laptop is part of your daily posture, comfort stops being cosmetic.

what gets missed is the tradeoff apple made visible. industrial design wants thinness, rigidity, and a silhouette you can recognize from across the room. users want a machine that disappears under their wrists. those goals overlap until they don't, and the overlap is where modern hardware quietly asks customers to absorb the cost.

that is why this story matters now, not because one person took a file to a macbook, but because more people are comfortable saying the official version is good enough while privately doing the ergonomic edits themselves. that's not personalization, that's unpaid product research.

if you want the signal, check the [hn comments](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47724352). the interesting part is not whether the hack is sane. it's how many assumptions people are willing to forgive once a device feels polished.
