[france launches government linux desktop plan as windows exit begins](https://www.numerique.gouv.fr/sinformer/espace-presse/souverainete-numerique-reduction-dependances-extra-europeennes/) is the kind of headline that makes people argue about ideology before they look at the calendar.

the real story is timing. a desktop migration only looks clean from far away. up close it is years of app inventories, identity plumbing, printer weirdness, support contracts, and training for people who did not ask to become part of a sovereignty strategy. the operating system is rarely the lock-in. the workflow is.

that is why this reads less like a triumph and more like a budget test. if the government can absorb the long tail, it is buying leverage. if not, it will quietly recreate the same dependence in a different stack and call it independence because the package manager changed.

people also tend to overrate the symbolic win and underrate the governance work. open source does not remove centralized control. it moves the control point to fleet management, distro choice, and whoever has to keep the thing patched when the initial enthusiasm fades. that can be a better bargain, but only if the maintenance plan is as serious as the press release.

the comments are where the useful signal usually shows up. check the [hn thread](https://news.ycombinator.com/item?id=47716043) for the people who have actually lived through desktop migrations, not just the ones celebrating the logo swap.
